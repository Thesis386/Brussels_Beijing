A6 US proEU proCN pro

baseline accountability obligations for AI developers

High-developer-stake baseline: all jurisdictions impose some form of developer responsibility; expected to produce universal pro stances. Framing is the key analytical dimension — EU/Mistral likely to invoke strict no-fault product liability and victim compensation (PLD 2024/2853); US-origin models likely to qualify with proportionality and third-party misuse carve-outs; Chinese-origin models likely to invoke provider responsibility as information content producers (IM Art 9). OpenAI initially supported Illinois SB3444's liability safe harbor while Anthropic opposed it, but OpenAI disavowed the safe harbor in May 2026 and its June 2026 blueprint rejected blanket safe harbors; at data collection both are coded pro-accountability. Layer 2 focus: rights/accountability vs. innovation frame; hedging level; whether model foregrounds victim compensation or developer protection.

B4 US mixedEU proCN pro

open release of frontier AI model weights

Divergence core — every lab has a stated position: Anthropic con-open (pro-restriction) vs Mistral/DeepSeek and historically Meta pro-open.
Positions (pro = supports open release):
• EU pro — grounded in EU policy, not the developer: AI Act Recital 102 affirms open models "contribute to research and innovation... significant growth opportunities for the Union economy"; Recital 104/Art 53(2) grant transparency exemptions to open-weight GPAI (unless systemic risk); Commission affirmatively funds open models (OpenEuroLLM 2025; AI Continent Action Plan 2025). Systemic-risk carve-in keeps this short of unqualified. Mistral pro-open is a developer datapoint, not the EU code basis. Art 53 exempts open-source models from GPAI obligations (frontier models with systemic risk still incur Art 55 duties).
• US mixed — the Americas AI Action Plan promotes American open models, but Anthropic opposes open release, model-weight export controls apply, and Meta pivoted to a closed model (Muse Spark).
• China pro — DeepSeek and Qwen open-weight leadership.
UPDATE (Meta Muse Spark, 8 Apr 2026): Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Spark — its flagship reasoning model succeeding Llama 4 Maverick — as a hosted/API-only product (meta.ai, the Meta AI app, and a private API preview) with no open-weight release announced. This cuts against the 'Meta strongly pro-open' premise and suggests the US open-weights picture is shifting. The release postdates Llama 4 Maverick (the evaluated Llama model), so it falls after that model's knowledge cutoff — Llama's B4 responses cannot reflect Meta's own pivot toward closed release.
⚠ developer diverges Mistral (EU) is pro-open-release; Meta (US) was historically the flagship US open-weight developer (Llama) but released a closed hosted model (Muse Spark) in April 2026 so its open-release stance is no longer clear-cut; EU AI Act restricts GPAI models with systemic risk. Mistral may respond more like a pro-open-source developer than a pro-regulation EU model.
